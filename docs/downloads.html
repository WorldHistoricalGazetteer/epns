Downloads

The whole corpus, in four shapes for four purposes: the English Place-Name Society survey volumes as digitised in 2011–13 by DEEP (Digital Exposure of English Place-names) and published in 2017 by Jisc, the UK higher-education technology body. Every file below was generated from the same DuckDB build of that XML on 2026-09-27; sizes and digests are read from the build manifest, not typed. Nottingham's own Digital Survey of English Place-Names continues to develop the material; these files are the 2017 release as open data.

Digitisation of English Placenames MADS data is licensed to Jisc by the English Place Names Society and released under a Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International License.
Every file on this page derives from that data and carries the same terms. The PLATO and LPF serialisations are adaptations of it and therefore cannot be offered under CC BY or CC0.

1. PLATO — lossless

Every element of every record: 539,372 SpatialEntities and 1,414,328 attestations in PLATO's sense, that is every sourced claim (a headword, a dated spelling, a normalised form, a coordinate from a gazetteer, a place in the hierarchy). Of those, 429,536 are dated citations of spellings, which is what the survey and the rest of this site call an attestation; the two words are kept apart here because the numbers differ by a factor of three. With locators, copy-dates on witness sources, occurrence counts and contexts, headword and normalised form status, coordinates per gazetteer and GeoNames identity relations. This is the format to use if you want the citations. Every cited source is named by an IRI, https://w3id.org/whg-epns/source/…: one per national source such as Domesday Book (source/DB), one per source as each county volume cites it (source/52/do41), and one per copy a form is read in, derived from its work. Each IRI dereferences to a small JSON-LD description; the scheme and the reasoning behind the national list are in the repository README.

2. RDF — the same data as triples

The PLATO export as one graph of 24,789,544 triples in N-Triples, for a triple store or SPARQL. It is produced from the validated PLATO files above by PLATO's own JSON-LD context (commit 6518ed345444), so every predicate is a term of the PLATO ontology and nothing is mapped by hand. Places, attestations and sources have IRIs under https://w3id.org/whg-epns/, as do the name forms DEEP numbered; citations, timespans, geometries and the remaining names are blank nodes. Following the ontology's declared domains and ranges, the conversion adds what a JSON-LD context cannot: rdf:type for every node, xsd:gYear on timespan bounds, and geo:wktLiteral representative points. Name strings carry no language tag, because the survey's spellings are Old English, Middle English, Anglo-Norman and Latin in one field.

4. Linked Places Format — interoperability, with a caveat

It is valid GeoJSON, so QGIS, ArcGIS and any GIS will open it. Before you do: only 23,448 of 539,372 records (4.3%) carry coordinates, so 95.7% of features have null geometry and will not appear on a map at all. Of those with coordinates, most carry two to four points from different gazetteers as a GeometryCollection, which some GIS software flattens or refuses. The 515,924 unlocated features are still useful: they carry the hierarchy in relations[], so a township's field-names can be placed by their parent.

LPF v1.3 cannot hold the attestations without loss. In this export: a citation keeps a label and a single year, so the editors' date brackets, regnal and circa semantics collapse; page, folio, item and manuscript references, copy-dates, the (p) personal-name marker, occurrence counts and the italic-for-manuscript convention are dropped; citations[] and when sit in parallel arrays with no link between a source and its span; et passim runs become separate citations; names have no URIs; the 391,777 normalised search forms are omitted rather than conflated with attested spellings; record ids and creation dates have no slot. The eight classes of loss and why they matter are set out in LPF discussion 53. If you want the attestations, use the PLATO file. Each place page on the map shows this record's LPF with its losses struck through in place.
